The challenge

Simple questions. Days to answer.

Cost reports, security scores, advisor recommendations, health signals and policy results each sit in a separate portal. When a manager asks a straightforward question, someone opens four or five tools and reconciles the results in a spreadsheet. These questions should take minutes.

Visibility and ownership

  • What cloud resources do we have?
  • Who owns them?
  • What are they costing us?
  • Which applications or teams need attention?
  • What changed recently?
  • Is our cloud estate compliant and healthy?

Cost, risk and progress

  • Why did our cost increase?
  • Which resources create the most security risk?
  • Which recommendations need immediate action?
  • Where are the biggest optimisation opportunities?
  • Are identified savings actually being realised?
  • Who is accountable for closing each finding?
What the industry data shows

More FinOps teams haven't stopped waste from rising.

  • 29%of IaaS and PaaS spend is estimated to be wasted, up from 27%: the first rise in five years
  • 85%of organisations name managing cloud spend as a top challenge
  • 63%now run a dedicated FinOps team, and 71% a cloud centre of excellence

Source: Flexera State of the Cloud 2026.

The large, obvious savings have often been taken already. What's left is a long tail of smaller opportunities spread across many resources and many owners, while FinOps teams stay lean. A recommendation with no owner, no deadline and no way to confirm the result usually stays open, however good the dashboard that shows it.

The problem is not a lack of data. It is the gap between data and accountable action.
The cost of standing still

The same issues, again and again.

  • Savings are identified but never implemented, or implemented but never measured.
  • Security findings age quietly because nobody is clearly responsible for them.
  • Cost spikes are discovered on the invoice, weeks after they began.
  • Unowned resources and incomplete tags make allocation and chargeback unreliable.
  • Leadership reports are assembled by hand, and each carries slightly different numbers.
  • Recommendations are dismissed informally, with no record of who accepted the risk or for how long.

Capabilities

Cost intelligence

Most cost tools show what was spent. MyOps explains why it changed. For any scope it compares this month with last month, shows absolute and percentage variance, and places the result in 3, 6 and 12-month trends, a year-on-year comparison and a forecast. Every cost driver is backed by evidence, so a variance can be explained to finance with confidence.

  • Current month and previous month
  • Absolute and percentage variance
  • 3, 6 and 12-month trends
  • Year-on-year comparison and forecast
  • Resource group, service and resource movement
  • Newly appearing services and cost-producing resources

Cost anomaly centre

Waiting for the invoice is the slowest way to find a cost problem. MyOps compares spend with an expected baseline and raises unusual behaviour automatically: unexpected increases, new services generating spend, new expensive resources, sudden resource-level jumps, and cost linked to incomplete ownership data.

  1. Baseline
  2. Current cost
  3. Variance
  4. Contributing scope
  5. Investigation

Budget, actual and forecast

Cloud operations and financial planning work from the same numbers. See budget, actual spend, forecast and variance, in absolute and percentage terms, for any scope, with the cost trend alongside. From a variance at portfolio level, drill down to the subscriptions, services and resources responsible for it.

FinOps optimisation

Optimisation works best as a continuous practice, not a quarterly clean-up. Opportunities from several sources land in one list, ranked by potential value and shown in context: who owns the resource, which application it supports and what else is happening around it.

  • Provider optimisation recommendations
  • Idle and underutilised resources
  • Rightsizing candidates
  • Unattached and unused infrastructure
  • Cost anomalies
  • Governance-related cost issues

Savings register

Finding a saving is not the same as achieving it. Each opportunity follows a clear lifecycle, with the estimated saving, baseline cost, implementation date, new run rate, evidence, validation result and realised saving recorded along the way.

  1. Identified
  2. Approved
  3. Implemented
  4. Validated

Potential, implemented and validated savings are reported separately, so financial reporting stays honest and leadership gets numbers it can rely on.

Security and risk management

Security findings are most useful when tied to the resource, the application and the person who can fix them. Critical, high, medium and low findings are shown with affected resources and applications, owners, age, remediation status, approved exceptions and evidence. Findings stop being another dashboard and become work that is assigned, tracked and closed.

Recommendation action centre

Security, FinOps, optimisation and governance recommendations arrive in one queue. Every action records the resource, application, owner, source, severity, potential saving, assignee, due date, notes, evidence, exception status, change history and final result.

  1. New
  2. Triaged
  3. Assigned
  4. In progress
  5. Resolved
  6. Verified

At any moment you can answer four questions: what needs action, who owns it, when it's due, and whether the fix has been verified.

Recommendation ageing and SLA

Each recommendation is tracked by severity, age, SLA target, owner, application, status and due date, and the items that need attention are highlighted straight away. Managers get a clear, factual basis for review meetings.

  • OverduePast the agreed SLA
  • Approaching SLADue date close
  • UnassignedNo owner yet
  • CriticalHighest severity

Exception management

Some recommendations can't be implemented straight away, because of a business dependency, a planned migration or a deliberately accepted risk. Each exception keeps its reason, owner, approval state, expiry date and history. When it expires, the finding returns to the queue, so a temporary decision never becomes a permanent blind spot.

  1. Requested
  2. Approved or rejected
  3. Expired or withdrawn

Governance and data quality

Good operations depend on trustworthy metadata. MyOps continuously evaluates ownership, application association, environment, department and allocation data, contact information, policy compliance, cost allocation, currency consistency, data freshness and source quality. A dedicated Data Quality Centre shows exactly where information is incomplete, so it can be fixed at the source.

Cloud resource health

See unhealthy resources immediately and understand their impact. Health rolls up to application and owner level and stays connected to cost, security and recommendations, so a team can see at once whether an unhealthy resource is also expensive, exposed or already under action.

Change timeline

When something goes wrong, the first question is: what changed? The timeline records resource and configuration changes, ownership and metadata changes, cost movement, new recommendations, security posture changes and health changes. Choose a historical window and drill straight into the affected resource.
